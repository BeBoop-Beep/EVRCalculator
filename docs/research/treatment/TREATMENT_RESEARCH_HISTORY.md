# Treatment research history

## Recovered lineage

Treatment V1 was conceptual: older desirability notes proposed a Treatment Prestige input, but no production formula or approved score authority was found.

Card Treatment Prestige V2 (`card_treatment_prestige_v2`) created the first explicit schema and resolver. It separated rarity designation, printing finish, special treatment, edition, era and supertype, joined exact pull probability, and attempted scarcity-controlled price models plus same-card/common-support contrasts. Price was the outcome, not a taxonomy input. The causal approval gate failed: common support was local and sparse, canonical joins needed reconciliation, and the work explicitly corrected “identification” to “common support.” Verdict: `DO_NOT_APPROVE_CARD_TREATMENT_PRESTIGE_V2`.

Treatment Market Prestige V3 was an observational market program, not collector-preference authority. It decomposed treatment components and controlled set, species and mechanics, but used Near Mint market price as its outcome. Across 24 rounds it added frozen cohorts, regime/era models, temporal checkpoints, coverage recovery, supporter/trainer domains, historical panels, analogue graphs and grouped ladders. Some era-local market associations were stable, especially Scarlet & Violet; other eras remained insufficient, unstable or structurally unsupported. Its retained verdict was partial observational support, never causal preference authority.

The later price-independent review retained V2/V3’s structural decomposition but found no clean public, rarity-blinded collector-preference dataset. Treatment therefore remained `DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY`. Exact Pull Scarcity, rarity designation, visual treatment, subject appeal and Artist Appeal are separate constructs; market price cannot identify Treatment Preference without reintroducing their confounding.

## What was rejected and retained

Rejected: global rarity ladders; treating “Secret Rare” as one cross-era finish; expensive-equals-premium reasoning; scarcity-derived treatment scores; transitive pair ordering without an explicit model; causal language from price regressions; promotion into Collector Appeal.

Retained: normalized composable identity; era-first comparison; exact variant identity; same-card and local common-support contrasts; explicit null/ambiguity states; scarcity as a diagnostic/control; price only as a later outcome; deterministic frozen cohorts and fingerprints.

The unanswered T2 question is whether collectors prefer presentation attributes after subject, artist, mechanics and scarcity are controlled using price-independent evidence. T1 supplies structural cohorts only and assigns no points.

## External terminology checks

The Pokémon Company’s [Scarlet & Violet rarity-system announcement](https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokemon-news/pokemon-tcg-scarlet-and-violet-revamps-pokemon-tcg-card-aesthetic) explicitly distinguishes Double Rare, Ultra Rare, Illustration Rare, Special Illustration Rare and gold Hyper Rare, supporting era-local labels rather than a timeless ladder. Its [HGSS Triumphant checklist](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms/pdf/tcg/checklists/HGSS4_Cardlist_Lo.pdf) separately identifies Prime and LEGEND rarities. Official rules describe [Radiant Pokémon as Shiny cards with a special rule](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2/pdf/trading-card-game/rulebook/swsh10_rulebook_en.pdf), confirming that “Radiant” bundles presentation and mechanics. Bulbapedia’s [rarity history](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rarity) was used as a secondary historical cross-check for LV.X, Prime, LEGEND, Amazing Rare and Radiant terminology.
